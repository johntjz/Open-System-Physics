### 1. Beyond Guesswork: The Mathematics of Jing Fang

In classical *Shanghan Lun* prescribing, effectiveness relies entirely on strict proportional ratios (*Bi Li* 比例). Randomly throwing a handful of herbs into a pot ignores the precise topological balance required to shift an open thermodynamic system. 

### 2. The Ying-Wei Ratio (Gui Zhi to Bai Shao)

Take the foundational Ying-Wei harmonization matrix: *Gui Zhi* (桂枝) is deployed at a ratio relative to *Bai Shao* (白芍) typically sitting around 1:1.5 to 1:2.5 (e.g., 9g of *Gui Zhi* to 15g of *Bai Shao*). *Gui Zhi* opens the defensive *Wei* layer and warms the channels, while a higher proportion of *Bai Shao* astringes the nutritive *Ying* layer and builds fluid reserves. If you flip or ignore these ratios, the thermodynamic vector collapses—either scattering the patient's energy too violently or trapping cold inside.

### 2. 營衛比例（桂枝與白芍）

以基礎的營衛調和矩陣為例：**桂枝**與**白芍**的相對比例通常維持在 1:1.5 至 1:2.5 之間（例如 9g 桂枝配 15g 白芍）。桂枝開啟防禦性的**衛**分並溫通經脈，而較高比例的**白芍**則收斂營分並建立津液儲備。若顛倒或忽略這些比例，熱力向量便會崩潰——要麼過於猛烈地發散患者能量，要麼將寒邪死鎖於內。
